Spreadsheet Route Guide

Follow the manual reading path from a Sheet cue to the current source page — and re-check options, size, price, and seller terms along the way.

The route, step by step

A LitBuy spreadsheet row is a cue, not a destination. The route from that cue to a decision is manual: you open the link, read the page you actually land on, and re-check the details against your selection.

1. Read the Sheet cue

Start with what the row actually is: a reference someone compiled. Note its origin — the shared Sheet, a category grouping, or a link you copied yourself. The closer a cue sits to the source page, the less it depends on someone else’s snapshot.

2. Open the source page

Follow the link and read the page you actually reach. Check its title, its domain, and where the link finally resolved. Redirects and changed listings can move you without warning.

✓Confirm the page matches the cue before reading any further detail.

3. Re-check the options

Color, size, and version each change which unit you are looking at. Re-select your option and re-read what that option shows.

4. Re-read the price in context

A price only means something inside an option. Fix the options first, then read the price against the exact selection — including quantity.

5. Check the seller terms

Terms, return conditions, and what the seller states on the page are part of the source-page check. Read them where they appear rather than assuming they match the Sheet.

?Open variable: does the page you opened match the item the Sheet cue described?

A worked reading

Consider a Sheet row that lists a jacket.

  1. Sheet cue: the row is compiled in the shared Sheet and grouped under a category. It is an entry.
  2. Source page: opening the link lands on a store page whose title names the jacket, and the domain matches the route you expected.
  3. Options and price: the color, size, and price shown must be re-read against your selected option before anything is settled.

Decision-ledger example — not an order, quote, QC approval, or live shipment.

When the route pauses

Pause when the page identity does not match, when an option conflicts, or when a variable stays unresolved. Each is a signal to go back one step rather than move forward.
